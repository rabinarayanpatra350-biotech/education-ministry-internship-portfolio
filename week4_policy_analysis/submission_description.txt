This Week 4 report of my Education Ministry internship in Coimbatore is a full policy analysis of India's foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) arc, covering all six assigned objectives. It begins by researching the policy landscape across its four layers — the NEP 2020 vision declaring FLN the highest priority, the NIPUN Bharat Mission of July 2021 with its grade-wise Lakshyas, Samagra Shiksha financing of over Rs 6,000 crore, and Tamil Nadu's Ennum Ezhuthum implementation reaching 25.08 lakh students — synthesized by the NITI Aayog policy roadmap of May 2026. The effectiveness analysis then applies three tests using verified ASER 2024, NAS 2021, and PARAKH data: learning moved (Class 3 government-school reading recovered from 16.3% to 23.4%, the highest ever measured), impact was equitable (government-school children gained 7.1 percentage points in reading against 2.4 for private schools), and delivery is incomplete (only 36.2% of schools received FLN-specific funds). Three state case studies follow — Tamil Nadu for design depth (a 16.5-percentage-point arithmetic gain), Himachal Pradesh for consistency, and Uttar Pradesh for scale. The report's centerpiece is a formal policy brief comparing three options through a scored table, recommending the extension of the mission to 2032 combined with level-based reforms, followed by a government database guide, a five-slide presentation plan for senior officers, honest limitations, and 14 references.
